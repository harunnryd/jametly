# Trademarks

The **"jametly"** name and any associated logos are **not** covered by the MIT license that applies to this codebase (see [LICENSE](./LICENSE)).

The MIT license grants you the right to use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, sublicense, and/or sell copies of the source code — subject to the standard MIT conditions. It does **not** grant you the right to use the "jametly" name, logos, or branding to identify a derivative product.

## What you may do (without our permission)

- Fork the code and modify it for personal, educational, or research use
- Reference this repository in academic papers, blog posts, or other OSS projects
- Use the name "jametly" in descriptive prose ("a fork of jametly that adds feature X")

## What you may not do (without our written permission)

- Publish a derivative work under the "jametly" name
- Use the "jametly" logo or branding on a derivative product
- Market a fork or wrapper as "jametly" or as endorsed by jametly
- Register a domain, package, or social handle that includes "jametly" to promote a commercial derivative

If you would like to use the "jametly" name or marks for a commercial derivative, contact the maintainers (see SECURITY.md for the disclosure email — same channel).

## Why this matters

Source-available vs. open source is a delicate distinction. Mirroring the pattern used by Bun, Astro, and the Linux Foundation, we want the **code** to be free for any use while keeping the **brand** as a signal of trust and provenance.

See:
- [Google Open Casebook: Trademarks in Open Source](https://google.github.io/opencasebook/trademarks/)
- [License != Trademark (Forrester)](https://www.forrester.com/blogs/open-source-doesnt-mean-a-trademark-free-for-all/)
